Small-city halo test: current 4km grid vs full-resolution (500m) VIIRS

The left maps use the exact rendering code and data shipped in the app today (4km fine patches). The right maps sample the same VIIRS Black Marble 2025 data at its native ~500m resolution with the same K1/K2/Walker skyglow model (K1=0.11, K2=0.05, d-3, 125km). Potsdam NY shows the halo problem; the Bakken oil fields in North Dakota show why naive full-resolution amplification is dangerous: gas flares look exactly like city cores. Each view is ~2.8x wider than the first version so the 4km halo can be judged in context (Potsdam spans 1.7° lat × 2.0° lon; Bakken 2.0° × 2.7°).

Potsdam, NY — current (4km)

Shipped grid. The city is a flat blue wash; no core, no gradient.

Potsdam, NY — full-res (500m)

Same data, native VIIRS resolution. The core appears: SQM 19.2 at center.

Bakken, ND — current (4km)

Shipped grid. Flares smeared into a broad B4/B5 regional glow.

Bakken, ND — full-res (500m)

Gas flares resolve as intense dots (SQM ~16.6) — indistinguishable from a city core.

What this shows. At 4km the Potsdam core is averaged away (21.4 everywhere — the halo is a flat overestimate of the surroundings with no bright center). At 500m the core is obvious (19.2, a 2.7-mag gradient). But the same 500m view turns Bakken gas flares into blazing SQM 16.6 dots that no radiance-only algorithm can tell apart from a small city.

Method. Full-res: VIIRS VJ146A4 2025 AllAngle Snow-Free sampled at native 15 arc-sec; art = 0.11 × local radiance + 0.05 × sqrt(Walker d-3 sum to 125km, computed on a 4km grid and bilinearly interpolated); SQM = 22 − 2.5·log10(1 + art/0.174). Current: the app's own tile renderer (src/lib/bortleOverlay.js) against the shipped region data. Colors use the app's exact Bortle ramp. This page is a visual test only — no pipeline or shipped data was changed.

Population data: European Commission, Joint Research Centre (GHS-POP) was evaluated as a flare/city gate in earlier experiments but is not used in these renders.
